Craft

The making, week by week

Handmade runs on the material's clock, not yours. A look at how a korai basket actually comes together, and why the timeline is worth planning your order around.

When we say a batch takes weeks, it is not a scheduling quirk. It is the honest length of the process. Knowing roughly how a basket comes together makes it easier to plan your order, and easier to see where the time, and the value, actually go.

It starts in the water

Our baskets are woven from korai, a riverside sedge grown in the wet fields of central Tamil Nadu, around Musiri and the Kaveri belt. The reed is cut, split fine by hand, and then soaked, sometimes for many days, until it is supple enough to weave. The famous Pattamadai mats of Tirunelveli, the tradition our weave descends from, split the reed to a remarkable fineness and process it for weeks before a single strand is worked. You cannot hurry water and fibre.

Dyed, then dried

Any colour goes in at the strand, before weaving, never printed on after. Reed is dyed in small lots and dried in the sun, which is why our palette stays in the range that natural dyes do best: oatmeal, rust, faded indigo, soft green. The drying, again, takes the time it takes.

Woven in small lots

Then the weaving, which is slow by nature and slower still done well. A single fine piece can take a weaver the better part of two to three weeks; a batch of a few hundred is woven across a cluster of hands, in small lots, over several weeks. This is why we work to order rather than from a warehouse. Nothing exists until your date is set, and each piece carries the small, honest variations of having been made by a person.

You cannot hurry water and fibre. The timeline is the craft, not a delay.

Finished by the same hands

At the end comes the part you actually see: the cotton band stitched on, the tassel knotted, the piece filled, personalised and checked. It is deliberately the shortest stage, because everything before it, the growing, splitting, soaking, dyeing and weaving, is where the weeks and the worth really are.

What it means for your order

Only one thing, really: start the conversation early. Eight to ten weeks before your day leaves room for the material to be itself, for samples, and for a calm delivery well ahead of the week of the wedding. Handmade cannot be rushed, but it can absolutely be planned. Tell us the date and we will work back from it.

Made to order in the weeks before your day. Tell us the date and we will shape the gift.
