Materials

Madras checks, the cloth we wrap in

The humble checked cotton we fold into every basket, and the long story woven into it.

Look inside one of our baskets and you will usually find a folded square of checked cotton. It is not a random choice. That cloth has a name and a history: the Madras check, woven on the Coromandel coast for a very long time.

Real Madras

The genuine article, once called the Real Madras Handkerchief, was woven from yarn dyed before it reached the loom, so the colours crossed and bled softly into one another as they were worked. Indigo over turmeric made a muddy green. Madder gave the warm reds. Each length carried the small irregularities of a hand at a pit loom.

It travelled far. Traders carried it across the sea, where it was worn as headwrap and wrapper, and it came back into fashion again and again. But it never stopped being made here, in the clusters around Madurai, Erode and the coast.

Let the material be the ornament.

Why we use it

We keep the palette quiet: oatmeal, faded indigo, terracotta. No loud motifs, just the weave itself and the honest slubs of hand-loomed cotton. Folded into a basket it does two jobs. It lines the gift, and it is a small keepsake of its own: a napkin, a wrap, a coaster that outlives the occasion.

Like everything we make, it is chosen to be used, not admired once and put away.
