Planning

How to plan your return gifts

Return gifts are the one part of a wedding your guests actually take home. A practical guide to planning them without the last-minute scramble: when to start, how many to order, what to spend, and how to keep hundreds of them feeling considered.

Almost everything about a wedding is spent on the day itself. The return gift is the rare part that leaves with your guests and stays in their homes, and it is also the part most often left to the final week. Anything handmade rewards a little planning. Here is how to do it calmly.

Start earlier than feels necessary

Anything made by hand runs on the material's clock, not yours. Reed is soaked, cotton is dyed, and a batch of a few hundred baskets is woven in small lots over weeks, not days. As a rule, begin the conversation about eight to ten weeks before the event. That leaves room to see samples, settle the details, and still have everything delivered comfortably ahead of the day rather than the night before it.

Count by household, then add a buffer

Guest lists are counted in people, but gifts are usually given by family, one to a household or a couple, so your gift count is often smaller than your headcount. Work out that number first. Then add ten to fifteen percent for the guests who arrive unannounced, the relatives who expect a slightly nicer one, and the few you will want to keep. Running a little over is a small cost. Running short, in front of the people who came, is the thing every host dreads.

Set a budget per gift, not just a total

It is easier to make good decisions per gift than in one large sum. Decide what a single gift should be worth, multiply by your count, and you have both a total and a lever. If the number comes out high, there are two honest ways down: make the vessel a little smaller, or simplify what goes inside. Neither has to cheapen it. A small, well-made basket with two considered things in it reads better than a large one packed with filler.

A small basket with two considered things in it beats a large one full of filler.

Anchor hundreds of gifts to one idea

The difference between a gift that looks designed and one that looks bought in bulk is usually a single anchor: one colour that runs through them all, one material, one repeated keepsake. Choose that before the details. It is what lets a few hundred gifts feel like a set rather than a shopping trip, even when no two are filled quite the same.

Make one thing personal

You do not need to personalise everything. One touch is enough: a name, a date, or a single line of your own on a tag, added by hand. It is a small amount of effort that does a great deal of work, the clearest signal that a gift was chosen for the occasion rather than ordered for it.

A simple timeline

If you want a shape to work back from:

  • 8 to 10 weeks out. Enquire, share your date, count and colours, and ask for samples.
  • 6 to 8 weeks out. Approve the sample, confirm the final count, and the batch begins.
  • 2 to 4 weeks out. Production and filling, with a personal touch added by hand.
  • 1 to 2 weeks out. Delivered, so the boxes are in your hands before the week of the wedding.
